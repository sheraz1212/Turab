Every foggy night, this railwayman walks out onto the tracks, and straps bombs to the rails.
It's England, in the eighteen-forties. Steam trains thunder through the countryside, faster than anything on earth.
But when the fog rolls in, the drivers can't see a thing. Not the signals. Not the lamps. Not even a train stopped right ahead.
So a young engineer has an idea. Little bombs, the size of a coin.
His bosses think he's lost his mind. Explosives? On the track? Under a moving train?
But he clips one to the rail, and waits.
Out of the fog, a train comes roaring in. The wheel rolls over it, and bang!
The driver can't see a thing, but he definitely hears that. He slams on the brakes, and stops just in time.
You see, a bang travels where light can't. Fog can hide a lamp, but it can't hide a bang.
And here's the crazy part.
They still use them today. Modern trains, computer signals, and still, a tiny bomb on the track.
