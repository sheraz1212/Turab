In the eighteen-nineties, people were so scared of being buried alive, they put bells on their graves.
A string ran from the coffin, up through the dirt, to a little bell on top.
So when Edgar Pratt was buried, his bell came with him.
Two nights later, at three in the morning, it rings.
The neighbor rolls over. Probably the wind.
Then it rings again. Harder.
Soon the whole street is out on their porches. You go. No, you go.
Somebody drags the doctor out of bed. Relax, he says. Dead bodies move. I signed the paper myself.
But one porch stays dark. Edgar's wife is sitting by the door, with a shovel in her lap.
Because she knows that ring. Two short, two long. The signal they practiced.
You see, Edgar wasn't dead. He owed money to half the town, so he faked it. She was supposed to dig him up, and they'd run.
Instead, she just sits there. All night. Until sunrise, when the bell finally stops.
And here's the crazy part.
Edgar's debts got buried with him. And the house? All hers.
