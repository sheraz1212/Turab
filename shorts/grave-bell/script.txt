In the eighteen-nineties, Edgar Pratt was terrified of one thing. Being buried alive.
And he wasn't crazy. Back then, doctors sometimes got it wrong.
So Edgar built a special coffin. A string tied to his finger, running up through the dirt, to a bell on top of his grave.
If he ever woke up underground, one tug, and the whole town would hear.
Everyone laughed. A coffin with a doorbell?
Then one winter, Edgar died. And they buried him, bell and all.
Three nights later, in the middle of a snowstorm, the bell rang.
Half the town grabbed lanterns and shovels, and dug like crazy.
They threw open the lid. And Edgar was still very dead.
You see, after a body is buried, it shifts and settles. Just a little. Just enough to tug a string.
And here's the crazy part.
People still say "saved by the bell." But no safety coffin ever saved a single person. The bells just scared everyone half to death.
