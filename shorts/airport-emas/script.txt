Every night, a quiet worker sneaks onto the runway with a cart full of gray blocks.
At the very end, right before a giant cliff, he lays them down one by one.
But these blocks are weak. Step on one, and it crumbles like a cookie.
A truck rolls over them, and its wheels sink right in.
The airport boss sees it and turns bright red.
"You put garbage at the end of my runway? A plane hits this and people die!"
He slams a sign into the ground: "RIP IT OUT. MONDAY 8 AM."
But on Sunday, a passenger jet lands too fast in the rain.
Its brakes scream. The runway runs out. The cliff gets closer.
Then the wheels hit the soft blocks.
Crunch. Crunch. Crunch.
The jet sinks deeper with every row, slower and slower,
until it stops just three feet from the edge.
You see, this crushable concrete is built to break.
Every block that crumbles under the wheels eats up the plane's speed, like a giant sponge.
It's called EMAS, and real airports use it today.
On Monday, the demolition crew shows up and finds a jet sitting in the blocks.
So the boss tears up the sign, and orders more garbage.
