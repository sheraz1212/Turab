This young engineer just built his very first bridge, and it stops six inches short.
Not by mistake. He measured that gap twice.
His boss turns purple. "Did you run out of steel, boy?"
So the kid plugs the hole with two giant steel combs, their fingers sliding together.
Next door, a rival company builds a flawless bridge. No gaps. Welded solid from cliff to cliff.
It opens first, and fancy cars float across like butter.
On the kid's bridge, every tire hits the comb. Ka-thunk!
The newspaper gives it a nickname: "The Ka-Thunk Bridge." Everyone takes the smooth one.
But as spring turns to summer, something strange happens. The gap starts closing.
A sandwich in April. A pencil in June. A coin in July.
"Look!" the boss cheers. "It finally fits!"
Then comes the hottest day of the year. One hundred fifteen degrees.
The flawless bridge groans, and its middle bulges up like a giant speed bump.
Cars launch off the top and land on their bumpers.
You see, steel swells when it gets hot. On a long bridge, it can push out by several inches.
The kid's combs gave the steel room to stretch. The welded bridge had nowhere to go, so it bent.
By August, the line to cross the Ka-Thunk Bridge is a mile long.
