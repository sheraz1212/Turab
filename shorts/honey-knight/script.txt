Every morning, this prisoner pours honey all over his shoulders. The guards beg him to stop. The whole cell gets sticky, and he just smiles. Then he kneels and waits, perfectly still, while every fly in the castle shows up for breakfast. "Why your shoulders?" a guard moans. A fly walks right across his nose. He doesn't even blink. That's it. The captain rips the honey jar out of his hands and storms off with it. But the flies follow the honey. Up the stairs, down the hall, and straight into the royal feast. Into the king's soup. Into the queen's hair. And the king hates flies more than anything. He swings his sword so wildly he chops his own dinner in half. "Who brought these flies?" he roars. Everyone points down, through the grate in the floor, at the prisoner. The king storms into the dungeon, and everyone is sure that man is about to lose his head. The prisoner kneels. One fat fly on each shoulder. Smack, left. Smack, right. "Thank you, Your Majesty." You see, when a king touches a man on both shoulders with his sword, that man becomes a knight. And nobody keeps a knight in chains.
