You're Nico Rosberg. And the kid you grew up racing go-karts with? Lewis Hamilton.
Years later, you're teammates again, in the fastest car in Formula 1.
There's just one problem. Lewis keeps winning.
2014: he takes the world title. 2015: he takes it again.
Two seasons gone, and you're running out of time.
So you make a plan. If talent alone can't beat Lewis, you'll beat him everywhere else.
Step one: your mind. You hire a mental coach, and you meditate every morning and every night, until pressure feels quiet.
Step two: sleep. A sleep expert shifts your bedtime for every time zone, so jet lag never catches you.
Step three: your gloves. You switch to thinner ones, just to feel the clutch better at the start.
Step four: weight. You give up cycling, your favourite sport, because bigger legs are heavier legs, and every extra kilo slows the car.
Every change is tiny. Together, they add up.
2016 begins, and you win the first four races in a row.
Then, in Spain, on the very first lap, you and Lewis crash into each other. Both cars, out.
Lewis comes roaring back. Race after race, the gap shrinks, and by the summer break, he's nineteen points ahead.
Then comes Japan. You take pole by thirteen thousandths of a second, and you win.
You're leading again. But Lewis wins the next three races.
It all comes down to Abu Dhabi. Lewis needs a miracle. You only need third place.
Lewis leads, and then he starts driving slower. On purpose. He's backing you into the cars behind.
Vettel is closing in. Verstappen is right there too. One mistake, and the title is gone.
But you hold on. You cross the line second.
After twenty-five years of chasing this dream, you're world champion, by five points.
Five days later, you stand in front of the cameras and announce your retirement. At thirty-one.
You walk away from the best car in the sport, and from the chance to win it again.
Because beating Lewis took everything you had. And once you'd done it, you never wanted to do it again.
