Every thirty-nine feet, this railroad worker cuts a gap in the track. On purpose.
It's the eighteen-hundreds, and his rival thinks he's an idiot. Gaps make trains bounce. Clickety-clack, all day long.
So the rival builds the perfect railroad. No gaps. Smooth steel, end to end, straight across the desert.
His trains glide in total silence. Passengers love it. The newspapers call it the future.
Then summer arrives.
The sun bakes the rails all afternoon, and steel does something sneaky. It grows.
One short rail only grows a tiny bit. But a whole mile of steel with no gaps? It needs almost two feet of extra room, and there's nowhere to go.
So the track shoves itself sideways, into a giant snake-shaped bend.
And the afternoon train is heading right for it.
You see, those little gaps were never mistakes. They were breathing room.
And here's the crazy part.
Most railroads today really have no gaps. Engineers stretch the steel tight and lay it on a hot day, so summer has nothing left to push.
