In the middle of Oakvale, there's a rusty lever, and the whole town is built around it.
Every street curls toward it in a giant spiral. And there's only one rule. Never, ever pull it.
For a hundred years, nobody does. Guards watch it day and night.
But Nora thinks it's ridiculous. So she digs through her grandpa's attic, and finds the founders' map.
The spiral streets aren't streets. They're drains. And Oakvale sits right in the middle of a dried-up river.
She shows the mayor. He laughs her out of the room.
Then one spring, the clouds turn black. It rains for nine days straight.
Up in the hills, the old river wakes up. A wall of brown water roars straight at the town.
Everyone runs. Nora runs the other way. Right past the guards, and she yanks the lever.
Deep underground, giant stone gates grind open.
The flood hits the spiral, spins around every house like water down a sink, and vanishes beneath the square.
Not one house floods.
You see, the founders never fought the river. They built the town to swallow it.
And here's the crazy part.
The rule was never "don't pull it." It was "don't pull it, until you need it."
