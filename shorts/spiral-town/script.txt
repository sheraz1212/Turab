In the middle of Oakvale, there's a rusty lever, and the whole town is built around it.
Every street curls toward it in a giant spiral. And there's one rule. Never, ever pull it.
Guards watch it day and night.
But Nora thinks it's ridiculous. So she digs through her grandpa's attic, and finds the founders' map.
The streets aren't streets. They're drains. Oakvale sits right on top of a dried-up river.
She shows the mayor. He laughs her out of the room.
Then one spring, it rains for nine days straight.
Up in the hills, the old river wakes up. A wall of water roars straight at the town.
Everyone runs. Nora runs the other way, right past the guards, and yanks the lever.
Deep underground, giant stone gates grind open.
The flood hits the spiral, spins around every house like water down a sink, and vanishes under the square.
Not one house floods.
You see, the founders never fought the river. They built the town to swallow it.
And here's the crazy part.
The rule was never "don't pull it." It was "don't pull it, until you need it."
