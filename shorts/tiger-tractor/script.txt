Nineteen forty-two. Germany rolls out the Tiger, the most powerful tank in the world.
It can hit an enemy tank from a mile away. Shells bounce off it like peas.
For every kill, the crew paints a ring on the barrel.
On the other side, the Soviets are building their T-34s in a tractor factory. Crude welds. Rough edges.
The Germans laugh. You're fighting us with tractors?
You see, the Tiger was built like a pocket watch. Every gear hand-fitted. Every part made for that one tank.
Then autumn comes. Mud. Then winter. One frozen morning, a Tiger's gearbox cracks.
A new one has to come all the way from Germany. That takes weeks.
Meanwhile, a T-34 loses a wheel. The driver walks to a wreck, pulls one off, and hammers it on.
It fits. Because every T-34 is the same underneath. So every broken tank is a parts shop.
Germany built about thirteen hundred Tigers. The Soviets built over fifty thousand T-34s.
So the Tiger kept winning fights, and kept losing the war.
And that Tiger with the cracked gearbox? Its crew is still waiting in the snow, watching the tractors roll past.
