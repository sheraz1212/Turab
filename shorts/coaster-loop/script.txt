Back in the 1890s, a builder makes a roller coaster that goes upside down. One perfect circle of a loop.
It costs him everything.
And of course, his rival copies it, bolt for bolt.
But the night before opening, his test dummy comes back crushed. Flat as a soda can.
He can't miss opening day. So he grabs a hammer, and bends the bottom of the loop into a long, lazy curve.
Does he know what he's doing? Not really.
His rival laughs. Then sneaks in that night, and cranks the sides together, until the loop is tall and skinny.
Opening day. He climbs into the first cart. As it tips over the hill, he finally sees the loop. Too late.
You see, a car can take a hairpin turn at five miles an hour. Take it at sixty, and it flips.
A coaster cart is fastest at the bottom of a loop, and slowest at the top.
A perfect circle puts the same sharp turn in both places.
But his lazy curve at the bottom, and that squeeze at the top? Exactly the right shape.
He sails over without a scratch.
Across town, the rival's cart hits the bottom of his perfect circle.
