# Short #02: How a Prison Hole Becomes the Great Escape Tunnel

This short follows the Cu Chi clone template ([Clone-Template_Cu-Chi-Breakdown.md](Clone-Template_Cu-Chi-Breakdown.md)) with the same beats, camera, caption style and rhythm. Only the place changes: jungle → **WW2 prison camp, pine forest, bright yellow sand**.

**Title:** How a Prison Hole Becomes the Great Escape Tunnel
**Length:** ~68 sec | 1080x1920 (tests 720x1280) | Voice: ElevenLabs (calm documentary)
**Pipeline:** Blender

> **Facts note** (confirm the numbers on Wikipedia, "Stalag Luft III" and "The Great Escape (1963 film)" is NOT the source):
> - Stalag Luft III was a German POW camp for Allied airmen at Sagan (today Żagań, Poland).
> - Huts stood on stilts so the guards ("ferrets") could look underneath. Seismograph microphones were buried along the fence to detect digging.
> - Three tunnels were dug: **Tom, Dick and Harry**. Tom was found and blown up; Dick was later used to dump sand.
> - Harry's entrance was under a stove in Hut 104. It went ~30 ft (9 m) down and ~336 ft (102 m) long.
> - The yellow subsoil looked different from the grey surface soil. "Penguins" carried it in bags inside their trousers and scattered it in the gardens.
> - The walls were shored with bed boards (thousands of them), and a hand pump made from kitbags pushed air through pipes made of milk tins (Klim). There was a rope-pulled trolley on wooden rails, and staging chambers called "Piccadilly" and "Leicester Square".
> - On 24–25 March 1944 the tunnel came up **short of the trees**, near a guard tower, in snow. 76 men got out.
> - **Monetization safety:** end on the breakout. Don't show or mention the executions that followed. No swastikas or flags; uniforms only.

## Voiceover script

> You're a prisoner of war, and you dig a hole to escape.
> But the guards crawl under every hut with flashlights.
> So you hide the entrance under a hot stove. Nobody looks there.
> Then they bury microphones in the ground to hear you dig.
> So you go down thirty feet. Too deep to hear.
> But the sand keeps collapsing.
> So you tear the boards out of your beds, and build walls.
> Now you dig sideways.
> But this deep, the sand is bright yellow. One grain on the grey ground, and they know.
> So you hide it in bags inside your trousers, and sprinkle it in the gardens.
> Down here, the air runs out. So you build a pump from kit bags, and pipes from milk tins.
> The tunnel gets so long that crawling takes forever. So you lay rails, and ride a trolley.
> Then the guards find your tunnel, and blow it up.
> But you didn't dig one tunnel. You dug three. Tom, Dick, and Harry.
> Harry stretches three hundred thirty-six feet, under the fence.
> On escape night, you break through the snow, and the tunnel comes up short of the trees. Right next to a guard tower.
> One by one, seventy-six men crawl out into the dark.
> Congratulations. You have built the Great Escape tunnel.

(~185 words, ~66–70 sec at speed 1.0–1.05)

## Blender build notes
- **Soil:** use a noise texture (Filmic/AgX view transform, not Standard) to avoid the cartoon look. Grey topsoil 0.3 m, yellow sand (#d9b44a range) with darker damp streaks.
- **New props:** hut on stilts, stove with glow, fence posts + wire, guard tower + spot light, bed-board frame (reuse box), bellows pump, rails + trolley, three tunnels, snow layer (white plane, shown only at the end), pine trees (cones).
